# Study: twin-01 — two screens, one clock, wrong (chain before code)

Direction: art-direction.svx 2026-09-13 "Two screens, one clock, wrong"
(Joel-approved): same source on two surfaces, one running at 0.9x; watch
them drift and re-sync; how long is the loop.

## The chain

**Source finding:** the clock lane's strongest verified evidence is
visible N-vs-N−180 motion mismatch at hard cuts (clock-02 retain,
clock-03 soak receipts). The provocation asks a different temporal
claim: not lag but RATE — two copies of the same 1943 cut at 1.

0x and 0.9x drift apart continuously and re-sync only by
intervention (a pointer cut restarts both). Loop length becomes
computable: the 0.9x copy falls a full source-duration behind every
~10 real plays; a "re-sync" is a designed cut, not an accident.

**Artistic question:** when the same film runs at two rates on two
surfaces, which copy is "the archive" — the one that matches the
audience's wall clock, or the one that is slowly going wrong?

**Material:** Conquerb1943 cut, one file, two `<video>` elements with
playbackRate 1.0 and 0.9 (no new pulls; the difference is entirely a
playback parameter).

**Three.js operation:** two slightly separated planes (the twin
surfaces), left at 1.0, right at 0.9. Pointer cuts (existing
poll/PUT path) hard-reset BOTH to frame 0 with a small offset stagger
so one copy visibly "catches up". A drift meter (small o11y event only,
`twin01.drift {seconds}` every heartbeat) records the growing gap.

**Observable results:** visible desynchronization compounding over
minutes; after N minutes the right surface shows a wholly different
scene from the left; a pointer cut re-syncs them with a visible catch-up
ramp. Loop length answer goes in the ledger as a measured number, not a
claim.

**Lineage:** branches clock-02 (`clock-02-076405c`, retained) — same
material, same same-view comparability; swaps lag (single surface,
delayed memory) for rate (twin surfaces, both live). Feeds the
surface-as-sculpture question: two physically separated surfaces are
already a split of the film into sculpture, at zero geometry cost.
