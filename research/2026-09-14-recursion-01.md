# Study: recursion-01 (recursive projection / three-layer frame)

## Chain

- **Source finding**: William Raban, *2'45"*, 1973, B&W 16mm (LUX, lux.org.uk/work/245, accessed 2026-09-14, verified): "An important aspect of *2'45"* is that it records the history of its making. It is a 'time-lapse' film in the sense that within its 2 minutes 45 seconds duration, it reveals all its past presentations as a film of a film of a film…". Each screening films the audience watching the previous night's projection; re-filming generates a natural positive/negative alternation (resettheapparatus.net). Raban's own proposition: the work "brought together the time of production with time of exhibition" (Senses of Cinema, Nautical Twilight review). Carried inside the window by the 2007 Teaching & Learning Cinema digital reenactment ("Six Minutes", 7th iteration, teachingandlearningcinema.org) — the recursion kept working 34 years later. What the source establishes: stacking temporal generations in ONE image is a legible historical practice, not an echo effect. What stays uncertain: how legibility survives when the generations are machine-speed (24-frame lag) instead of day-speed.
- **Artistic question**: can one frame carry three distinguishable temporal layers — the source *now*, the surface's *retained past*, and a *live pointer cut* that just happened — with no captions doing the work? (Art direction 2026-09-14.)
- **Material**: `videos/starter.mp4` (made here, catalogued sha256 8f74b70e…, see provenance) as the source fragment; `archive/content/current.json` (this project's own runtime pointer) as the live event feed — a clock cut we do not render ourselves.
- **Three.js operation**: three surfaces. (1) Source plane: starter video texture, direct. (2) Memory surface: pastCamera delay ring from 5135d94 (knot's frame n−24). (3) Intervention surface: a plane textured with a 2D-canvas rasterization of the pointer document (sha as stripe code, updatedAt as ticks), redrawing on each 60s pointer poll; a full-plane flash ramp fires on any pointer change. CanvasTexture per Ikeda's data-as-image move (source map, unverified entry — flagged in chain).
- **Observable result to examine**: over ~130s a viewer can see (a) gradient movement on the source plane, (b) the knot's retained past trailing on the memory surface, (c) the pointer plane change stripes exactly once when a pointer move lands, with a one-blink flash. Move the pointer via the approved MCP endpoint as the live test; if any layer is indistinguishable without reading the overlay, the study has failed its own question.
- **Lineage**: extends recurrence-01 (branch lane: recursion): keeps webgpu+o11y+starter clip+delay ring+pastCamera; changes adds source plane (blue) + live pointer surface + intervention flash; inspired-by antecedent Raban 2'45" (1973) via T&C Cinema 2007 reenactment.
