## The chain

**Source finding:** the clock lane's cuts so far are structural
(frame-difference) or operator (pointer). The provocation proposes a
linguistic cut: the 1943 film's own narration detected by ASR, where a
sentence boundary IS a hard cut. Second finding: the film exists in a
transcribed form on the Prelinger item page; whether to trust it or
regenerate locally is part of the study (regeneration is the
rephotography move — the archive's own transcript is generation n, ours
is n+1).

**Artistic question:** if a spoken word chooses the loop length, does
the film's voice become the editor — and is a machine transcript a
legitimate reading of the film or a second, separate work?

**Material:** Conquerb1943 (already in the lane, provenance clock-01);
the audio track is on the inline cut (verify it survived the -an

The existence of the item's own Whisper JSON makes the rephotography
question concrete and cheap: generation n = the archive's own ASR (pull

Conquerb1943.mp3. Schema VERIFIED 2026-09-14 against the live artifact

provenance; the study compares where the two transcriptions disagree

**Three.js operation:** whisper over the audio; each sentence boundary
becomes a loop candidate; the loop length is chosen by a sentence, not
frames: the footage plane loops the sentence's span while the memory
panel plays through continuously (the past never loops). Pointer cuts
re-seed which sentence is current.

**Observable results:** a visibly sentence-bounded loop (footage
returns at each spoken boundary while the clock-face/panel behaves
differently — the past refuses the loop); o11y `transcript01.loop
{sentence, spanMs}` per boundary; the transcript itself becomes the
source citation for loop choice, listed in the archive manifest.
