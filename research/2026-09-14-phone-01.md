## The chain

**Source finding:** the verified AD provocation (art-direction.svx, dateline
2026-09-14): "On a phone, device tilt drives the apparatus: orientation as
projector angle onto the surface, or acceleration as the delay offset, so
holding still means a stable image and moving means smear." Second finding:
Safari iOS fires no motion events until `DeviceMotionEvent.requestPermission()`
runs from a user tap — the archive web is full of broken sensor demos for
exactly this reason; the first touch must be designed INTO the work, not an
error path. What stays uncertain: android chrome/desktop fallback perception
until tested on real hardware.

**Artistic question:** When the viewer's own hand is the projector gate —
tilt steers what the surface shows, motion smears the delay — does holding
still become the study's gesture? Is stillness the work?

**Material:** same Conquerb1943 cut (provenance `clock-01-conquerb1943`,
CC PD), reused in place — the clock film that disciplined bodies now played
by the hand that owns the phone. Optional recorded motion trace for desktop:
a JSONL of {t, alpha, beta, gamma} captured from a real device session, kept
out of git if large (same handoff rule as clips).

**Three.js operation:** DeviceOrientation alpha/beta/gamma → camera rig
(orientation as projector angle: the "surface" plane rotates opposite the
hand, like aiming a projector). DeviceMotion accel magnitude → delay offset:
`DELAY_effective = DELAY_base * (1 + clamp(|a|, 0..2))` — moving smears the
past into the present; holding still locks the image. Trace replay feeds the
same rig code from a JSONL on a desktop frame parameter.

**Observable result:** within 5s of the permission tap, tilting the phone
visibly steers the surface; a shake visibly smears it; holding still freezes
it (heartbeat says which input drove the frame: `source: 'sensor'|'trace'|
'fallback-cursor'`). Verified by recorded-trace replay (headless can hold
the phone path); on-device check pending someone holding one.
