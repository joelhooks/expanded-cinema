# Study: colour-01 — the delay becomes chroma

Move: **Branch**. Branches withhold-01.3 (the wall holds the legible past):
same room, same screen, same seeked one-shot arrival — but the delay lineage
is now rendered as colour, and the room is lit by what the film splits.

## The chain (four named before the build — art-direction.svx, Joel 2026-09-15)

**Palette (with source).** The three channel primaries, projected light, not
pigment: Red `0xff2a1a`, Green `0x2bff6a`, Blue `0x2a6aff` — plus one named
warm for the room: Amber `0xffa02a`. Source: Lis Rhodes, *Light Music*,
1975 (Tate Tanks 2012; LUX) — two projectors facing each other, optical
sound, the audience standing inside the beams; the screen is the room.

**Light as the medium.** The projector becomes a saturated point light
(colour cycling through the channel primaries weighted by the film's own
channel lumas). Amber fog with a body (FogExp2) so every beam is a volume;
the walls appear where the beam lands. The iris ring keeps the arrival beat
but now flashes the three primaries in sequence at the cut.

**Geometry with relief.** The screen stays the curved withhold plate, but the
wall picture returns as a SECOND lit surface, and the floor stops being
mathematical: a displaced grid (200x120 segments) that catches the coloured
light with real falloff. The beam slices become three coloured cones (one per
channel), each animated with its own sway so the light itself moves.

**Node shaders as the tool.** ONE TSL node material carries the operation:
the screen samples the SAME video texture three times with per-channel time
offsets (0s, 1.5s, 3s — DELAY_R/G/B), taking only its own channel from each
sample. Still regions collapse to monochrome; motion fringes into colour.
Nothing else in the scene touches the operation.

## Artistic question

What does 1.5 seconds of delay look like when it is not grey shadow but
green and blue light on the walls? The withhold becomes chromatic: the past
is not darker, it is another colour.

## Material

`videos/conquerb1943.mp4` (same 1943 cut, shas in catalog.json); the withhold
room geometry budget (proven positions from the magenta probes).

## Three.js operation

TSL Fn on the screen material: `vec3(rgbSplit(UV))` = `vec3(tex(uv).r,
tex(uv - parallaxG, delay 1.5s).g, tex(uv - parallaxB, delay 3s).b)`.
Per-channel delay implemented with `time`-driven ring buffers? No: the video
element CANNOT rewind, so the delays are spatial parallax proxies in still
regions and true temporal splits need three offscreen captures. Method:
three TextureSamplers of the same VideoTexture cannot be time-shifted —
real per-channel delay requires three render targets sampled at n,
n-D1, n-D2. Use the clock-02 ring grammar: capture the luma-lit view to
three one-slot-lagged targets... Simplest honest build: three THREE.RenderTarget
ring buffers, caps written per frame by a hidden ortho camera rending the video
plane; screen TSL Fn samples RT_now.r, RT_45f.g, RT_90f.b (45f = 1.5s@30,
90f = 3s@30).

## Observable result

At the 8s frame (closed, pre-arrival): the amber fog body and the three
channel cones sweep the room — colour in the ROOM, not on a plate.
At 16s (3s past... here 3s pos-arrival... the fringes... updated during build): the open
screen fringes red/green/blue on any motion; the room's walls and floor
carry the cast colour. Frames at 8s and 16s are the gate.

## Razzle-dazzle gate (Joel, 2026-09-15)

Would a stranger scrolling past stop at the 5s frame? If the room is not
full-bleed coloured and the fringes do not reach the walls, no cut.
