```
Source finding:    Anthony McCall's solid-light films (Line Describing a
                   Cone 1973) made the beam a volumetric sculpture you
                   could walk through; haze turned projection into
                   architecture. Belson put volumes of light in deep
                   space. In this studio the beam already exists as
                   slats/slices; the melt study built the missing piece —
                   a feedback field that remembers.
Artistic question: Can the AIR between projector and surface carry the
                   same generations the surface carries — so the room's
                   light is made of the film's own past, not just its
                   present?
Material:          Unchanged — Conquer by the Clock (1943), the melt
                   study's clip; the cone branches from melt-01 v05
                   branch-by-copy, closed subtractive.
One variable:      The beam slices sample the SAME past-luma field the
                   relief displaces with (DataTexture, MELT_RELIEF_N
                   frames back) — each slice's brightness AND displacement
                   away from the optical axis are driven by the field at
                   that slice's arc fraction. The cone breathes with the
                   film's past.
Geometry:          The existing 120 slice sheets + 16 slats per beam;
                   slats gain a lateral offset (u-displacement along their
                   own normal) from the field. No new geometry classes.
Guard:             Displacement clamp 0.3 of the throw; the beam must
                   still land on the screen (rail audit assert stays).
Subtraction test:  Remove the field sampling from the slices → the beam
                   reduces exactly to melt-01 v05's beam. Nothing else.
Duration:          Melt rail dwell unchanged; the cone is visible across
                   the whole dwell, strongest under motion.
Observable:        Calm film → beam is a straight solid cone. Motion →
                   the cone bends/shimmers with the generations. Cut →
                   the air detonates WITH the surface (one event, two
                   bodies). Stillness → the air heals back straight.
Open questions:    - Does the cone read as a volume or a screen mistake?
                   - Is the air's memory the SAME time as the surface's
                     (one field, two bodies — the point), or does it need
                     its own horizon?
Verification:      5s/16s frames must show (a) straight cone at rest,
                   (b) bent cone under motion, (c) landing point still
                   on the plate under max displacement.
```
